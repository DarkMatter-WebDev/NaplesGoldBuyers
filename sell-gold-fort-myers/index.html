Straight Answer

Why call a Naples buyer from Fort Myers?

Fort Myers has its own gold buyers, and some of them are good. We're not going to pretend otherwise or invent a reason you should distrust them. Here's the honest case for picking up the phone anyway:

  • We come to you, free. No driving with valuables, no parking, no counter with other customers behind you.
  • Lower overhead means a tighter spread. We keep one small showroom and work largely by appointment rather than running a full retail operation. That cost has to come out of someone's offer, and it isn't coming out of ours.
  • You see the math. Weight, karat, spot price, written out — before the number, not after it.
  • Estates are normal here. A full collection isn't an inconvenience; it's most of what we do.

And the part most buyers won't put in writing: get another offer. Gold trades on a published market, so two quotes on the same chain are directly comparable. If a Lee County buyer beats our number, take it. We'd rather lose a deal than have you sell to us wondering whether you should have checked.

Older homes, older collections

A lot of what we see in Lee County comes out of houses that have been in the same family for decades — along McGregor, around Whiskey Creek, in the older parts of the city and out toward Fort Myers Beach. Those homes tend to hold the things that are easiest to undervalue: sterling flatware services nobody has polished since the eighties, coin collections assembled by someone who has since passed, unmarked gold from before hallmarking was consistent, and costume jewelry with two or three real pieces mixed in.

Don't sort it before we arrive. Sorting is the part we're good at, and the risk of guessing wrong runs entirely in one direction.

I have a whole estate. Can you handle it?
+

Yes, and it's most of what we do. Mention it when you call so we can plan a longer visit. We're comfortable working alongside an executor, estate attorney, or estate sale company.

Can I get a number before you drive up?
+

Usually a range, yes — text photos including any karat stamps. A firm offer needs weighing and testing in person, because photos can't establish weight or purity.

Should I clean or repair anything first?
+

No. Don't polish sterling, don't repair broken chains, and don't clean coins — cleaning a collectible coin can genuinely reduce its value. Bring it as it is.
