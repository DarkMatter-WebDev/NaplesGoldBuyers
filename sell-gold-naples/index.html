What It's Worth

Scrap gold pricing is arithmetic, not opinion

There are only three numbers in a scrap gold offer: how much it weighs, how pure it is, and what gold is trading for today. Multiply them together and you have the metal value. Any buyer who won't show you those three numbers is hiding one of them.

Purity is fixed by the karat stamp. It never changes, and it's the number most people haven't seen written down:

Gold content by karat — the fixed multiplier in every offer
Karat Pure gold content Pure gold in a 20 g piece
24k99.9%19.98 g
22k91.7%18.34 g
18k75.0%15.00 g
14k58.5%11.70 g
10k41.7%8.34 g

So a 20-gram 14k chain isn't 20 grams of gold — it's 11.7 grams of gold mixed with 8.3 grams of copper, silver, and nickel. That distinction is where most confusion about offers comes from. To see what that works out to at today's market, our spot price calculator does the conversion live.

Why two buyers quote different numbers

The metal value is objective. What varies is the spread a buyer keeps between their offer and the melt value — that's their margin, and it's the only part that's negotiable. A shop with a large storefront on Fifth Avenue, a sales floor, and a full staff needs a wider spread than we do. We keep overhead deliberately low and quote closer to melt, which is why people who shop three or four buyers tend to end up back here.

Bring everything — even the things you assume are worthless

The pieces people almost leave at home are frequently the ones worth the most:

  • Broken and single items — snapped chains, bent rings, one earring. Condition is irrelevant to metal value.
  • Dental gold — crowns and bridges are usually 16k or higher.
  • Class rings — heavy, and typically 10k or 14k.
  • Unmarked pieces — plenty of older and imported gold carries no stamp at all. We test it.
  • Gold coins and bullion — Krugerrands, Eagles, Maple Leafs, Sovereigns, bars and rounds.
  • Watch cases — solid gold cases have real weight, separate from the movement's value.

We also buy sterling silver, diamonds, luxury watches, and full estates. If you're clearing a house rather than selling one chain, say so when you call and we'll plan the visit differently.

FAQ

Questions we get every week

How much is my gold worth in Naples?
+

Weight × purity × live spot price. A 20-gram 14k chain holds about 11.7 grams of pure gold, since 14k is 58.5% gold. We weigh and test in front of you and show the arithmetic before making an offer, so the number is never a mystery.

Do I need an appointment?
+

No — walk in during posted showroom hours. If those hours don't work, or you'd rather not travel with valuables, call and we'll set a private appointment or come to you.

Do you buy broken or damaged gold?
+

Yes, and condition makes no difference to scrap value. A snapped chain is worth the same per gram as an intact one. Don't pay to repair anything before selling it.

What if my piece isn't stamped?
+

Common, especially with older and imported jewelry. We test it electronically and, when needed, with acid on a touchstone. Testing is free and non-destructive for the overwhelming majority of pieces.

How quickly do I get paid?
+

Immediately — cash in hand before you leave, or at your kitchen table on a house call. No waiting period, no check to clear.

Should I sell locally or mail it in?
+

Locally, almost always. You watch the scale and the test, ask questions, and decline on the spot if you don't like the number. Mail-in buyers hold your gold while you wait, and getting it back means a return shipment.

What should I bring?
+

The gold and a photo ID — Florida law requires us to record identification for precious metal purchases. Original boxes and papers help for branded jewelry and watches, though they're not needed for scrap.
